## Q1. Core sources, access methods (APIs), coverage, update cadence, and 2025–2026 changes/discontinuations

### Takeaway
Most core sources remain free and scriptable from Python, but 2025–2026 brought major disruptions: the **CIA World Factbook was discontinued on 4 Feb 2026** (directly affects the user's pipeline), **USAID funding of the DHS Program ended in Feb 2025** (archive/API kept alive on interim funding), **Freedom House scaled down Freedom in the World** after losing US government funding, the **IMF retired IFS and moved to an SDMX 3.0 portal**, and **WHO announced deprecation of the current GHO OData API (end-2025)**. Version bumps to note: V-Dem v16 (Mar 2026, data to 2025), PWT 11.0 (Oct 2025, ICP 2021 PPPs, to 2023), GDL SHDI v10.x (Mar 2026).

### Inferences

- IMF code paths written against the old `dataservices.imf.org` JSON / IFS codes are likely broken; prefer WEO bulk files or the new SDMX 3.0 endpoints (Python: `sdmx1` package supports SDMX 2.1/3.0 for IMF/OECD/ILO/ECB — background knowledge).
- WHO GHO code relying on `ghoapi.azureedge.net` should be considered at risk; a downloaded raw snapshot is the safest fallback.
- Freedom House continues but in reduced form; V-Dem is the more robust long-term choice for regime variables, while Polity V should not be extended past 2018.

## Q2. Data-quality issues (missingness, modeled vs observed, revisions/vintages, breaks, PPP rebasing, statistical capacity, manipulation)

### Takeaway
Missingness is not random: it concentrates in low-income, conflict-affected and small states and in countries with weak statistical systems (SPI scores rise with income). Many headline health and labour indicators are model outputs, not measurements. PPP-based series were rebased to ICP 2021 (WDI from May 2024; poverty lines from July 2025; PWT 11.0 in Oct 2025), so mixing vintages creates artificial level shifts. There is solid evidence (Martinez 2022, JPE) that autocracies overstate GDP growth.

### Cited Findings
- **PPP rebasing**: ICP 2021 results published 30 May 2024, with revised 2017 PPPs, a 2018–2020 PPP series and extrapolated 2022–2023 values; WDI adopted ICP 2021 PPP conversion factors in the May 2024 update; revised 2017 and 2021 results used a "standard approach" rather than the earlier reference-volume approach **[verified this session]** — [World Bank ICP data](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/icp/data); [ICP 2021 introduction](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/icp/brief/ICP2021_Introduction); [Data blog on revised 2017 PPPs](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/what-do-the-revised-2017-purchasing-power-parities--ppps--mean-f)
- Poverty: the World Bank moved global poverty lines to 2021 PPPs in the July 2025 WDI update; the extreme poverty line rose from $2.15 to $3.00/day — [WDI July 2025 update blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/world-development-indicators--july-2025-update---new-data-on-pov)
- PWT 11.0 incorporates the 2021 ICP and revised 2017 PPPs — [GGDC](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/blog/penn-world-table-version-11-0-is-published)
- **Statistical capacity (SPI)**: SPI framework assesses national statistical systems across 5 pillars (data use, services, products, sources, infrastructure) and 22 dimensions; covers 188 economies (up from 167 in 2016); 2025 release updates data through 2024 with full methodological comparability; statistical performance improves with income — [WDI Dec 2025 release note](https://datatopics.worldbank.org/world-development-indicators/release-note/dec-2025.html); [World Bank SPI 2025 document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099010211242513346/pdf/IDU-2d7d8351-fb03-4e10-ae72-ace1d416225b.pdf); SPI indicators in WDI (e.g., `IQ.SPI.PIL5`) — [data.worldbank.org](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/IQ.SPI.PIL5)
- **Data manipulation / GDP overstatement**: Martinez (2022), "How Much Should We Trust the Dictator's GDP Growth Estimates?", JPE 130(10): 2731–2769, DOI 10.1086/720458; compares reported GDP growth to satellite night-lights for 179 countries, 1992–2008; yearly GDP growth inflated by a factor of ~1.15–1.3 in the most autocratic regimes (≈35% overstatement — e.g., true 2% reported as ~2.7%); overstatement concentrated in components lacking third-party validation (public investment, government consumption) **[verified this session]** — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3093296); [BFI research summary](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/insight/research-summary/how-much-should-we-trust-the-dictators-gdp-growth-estimates/); [Harris Q&A](https://harris.uchicago.edu/news-events/news/qa-luis-martinez-exposes-way-dictators-exaggerate-economic-performance)
- **Index manipulation by institutions**: Doing Business irregularities (China, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Azerbaijan) led to discontinuation — [World Bank B-READY FAQ](https://www.worldbank.org/en/businessready/about-us/faq)
- **Poor Numbers**: Jerven (2013) documents weak GDP measurement in sub-Saharan Africa, e.g., Ghana's 2010 GDP rebasing raised GDP by ~60% overnight **[background, not re-verified]** — [Cornell University Press, Poor Numbers](https://www.cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801478604/poor-numbers/)
- **Modeled vs observed**: ILO unemployment for many countries are ILO modelled estimates **[background]** — [ILOSTAT modelled estimates](https://ilostat.ilo.org/methods/concepts-and-definitions/ilo-modelled-estimates/); WHO suicide rates and the UN MMEIG maternal mortality ratios are model-based estimates (Bayesian models filling gaps where vital registration is incomplete), and WHO flags many country values as modeled **[background, not re-verified]** — [WHO GHO suicide indicator metadata](https://www.who.int/data/gho/indicator-metadata-registry/imr-details/4664); [UN MMEIG trends in maternal mortality](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240108462)
- **Revisions/vintages**: GDL SHDI shows how a single dataset can have multiple point releases within months (v8.2→8.3→8.4→9.0→9.1 between Sep–Dec 2025, region counts changing 1,801→1,787→1,805) — [GDL archive](https://globaldatalab.org/shdi/download_files/). ALFRED (St. Louis Fed) keeps vintage histories for PWT and other series — [ALFRED PWT release](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/release?rid=285)
- **Survey-based well-being**: WHR rankings rely on ~1,000 Gallup respondents per country-year, averaged over 3 years, with confidence intervals that often overlap between adjacent ranks **[background]** — [WHR FAQ](https://worldhappiness.report/faq/)
- Survey data gaps: suspension of DHS fieldwork in 2025 interrupts the main source of household health/fertility data for many low-income countries — [DHS Program news](https://dhsprogram.com/Who-we-are/News-about-the-DHS-Program.cfm)

### Inferences
- In a 217-country × 2000–2024 panel, expect missingness to correlate with income, conflict and population size; listwise deletion will bias samples towards rich, stable democracies. Report a missingness matrix by World Bank income group, and include SPI as a control/moderator.
- Never mix PPP vintages: pull all PPP-denominated series from the same release (e.g., one WDI vintage, or PWT 11.0 only) and record the ICP base year (2017 vs 2021) in metadata.
- Flag modeled indicators (WHO, ILO modelled estimates, UN WPP for years after last census) in a metadata column so analysts don't treat them as independent observations; modeled values often share covariates (e.g., GDP) with right-hand-side variables, creating mechanical correlation.
- For autocracies, GDP growth regressions should consider robustness checks with night-lights or exclude/flag regimes with low V-Dem scores.
- Post-2025 DHS/USAID cuts likely increase future gaps in health and demographic indicators for low-income countries (inference — not yet quantified in any source found).

- Did not retrieve the WHO/UN MMEIG 2025 methodological documents to confirm current modeling details.
- No 2025–2026 literature found replicating or contesting Martinez (2022); not searched in depth.

## Q3. Harmonization (country codes, border changes, aggregates, crosswalk tools, time granularity, deflating/currency)

### Takeaway
Use ISO3 as the join key but maintain an explicit crosswalk table to M49, COW and Gleditsch-Ward codes; drop WDI regional/income aggregates before joining; handle successor states and Kosovo/Taiwan explicitly; and convert every monetary variable to one base (constant 2021 PPP or constant USD) from the same source vintage.

### Cited Findings
- V-Dem's country-year data is keyed on its own country_id plus COW codes and country names; ~179 countries — [V-Dem download](https://v-dem.net/data/the-v-dem-dataset/) (code-system detail from codebook — [background])
- Kosovo has no official ISO 3166 code; World Bank uses `XKX`, others use `XKO`/`KSV`/`RKS`; GDL had to release fixes for Kosovo data in v9.1 — [GDL archive](https://globaldatalab.org/shdi/archive/) (Kosovo code variants [background])
- Python crosswalk tools: `country_converter` (coco; converts between ISO2/ISO3/UN M49/COW/names, and handles regional classifications) and `pycountry` (ISO 3166 only); R `countrycode` package (covers COW, Gleditsch-Ward, ISO, M49, Polity, V-Dem, World Bank, etc. and many regex name patterns) **[background, not re-verified]** — [country_converter GitHub](https://github.com/IndEcol/country_converter); [pycountry PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pycountry/); [countrycode CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/package=countrycode)
- Gleditsch & Ward (1999) codes differ from COW for some states (e.g., Serbia/Yugoslavia continuity, German states) and are used by UCDP **[background]** — [Gleditsch state list](https://www.ksgleditsch.com/data-4.html)
- World Bank WDI API returns aggregates (regions, income groups, "World") mixed with countries; filter with the `region` metadata (aggregates have region "Aggregates") **[background]** — [WDI API country queries](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/898590)
- OWID includes its own aggregate entities (e.g., "World", "High-income countries", continents) with `OWID_` prefixed codes **[background]** — [OWID ETL docs](https://owid-docs.readthedocs.io/projects/etl/)

### Inferences
- Border changes in the 2000–2024 window that matter: Serbia & Montenegro split (2006), Kosovo (2008), South Sudan (2011, Sudan series before/after are not comparable), Curaçao/Sint Maarten (2010, Netherlands Antilles dissolved), Timor-Leste (2002). USSR/Yugoslavia/Czechoslovakia only matter if linking to pre-1992 V-Dem/COW/Maddison data.
- Join at country-year; aggregate sub-annual data (IMF monthly CPI, ILO quarterly) with documented rules (annual average vs end-of-period) and keep a flag. V-Dem scores reflect 31 Dec; WHR scores are 3-year rolling averages — label as such to avoid timing mismatches.
- Deflating: prefer source-provided constant-price series (WDI `NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD` = constant 2021 international $) over manual deflation; if converting currency, use the same source's exchange rates (WDI `PA.NUS.FCRF`) and document whether market or PPP rates.

- Could not verify current versions of country_converter / countrycode or their handling of Kosovo in 2026.
- No source retrieved on alliance/treaty datasets (ATOP, COW IGO, DESTA) current versions and their code systems (COW-based) — outside searched scope.

## Q4. Licensing, citation norms, and reproducibility

### Takeaway
WDI and OWID are CC BY 4.0; V-Dem, WHR, QoG and others require specific citation (with dataset version/DOI); Gallup raw data is commercial. Reproducibility needs dated raw snapshots, version/DOI recording, and per-variable metadata, because sources revise and even disappear (Factbook).

### Cited Findings
- World Bank data are licensed CC BY 4.0 (with some third-party exceptions noted in indicator metadata) **[background, not re-verified]** — [World Bank data licence](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/public-licenses)
- V-Dem v16 has a DOI (10.23696/vdemds26) to cite along with the codebook and methodology papers — [V-Dem v16 release statement](https://v-dem.net/release-statement-for-v16-of-the-v-dem-dataset/)
- WHR data: third-party mirror reports that data are open but users must reference both the World Happiness Report and the Gallup World Poll; dashboard/maps listed as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 **[secondary source only]** — [datahub.io mirror](https://datahub.io/@rufuspollock/world-happiness-report). Gallup World Poll microdata are a separate commercial product under an EULA — [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-uapupqnfizgci)
- PWT 11.0 is archived on Dataverse with DOI 10.34894/FABVLR — [GGDC PWT 11.0](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/productivity/pwt/pwt-releases/pwt110)
- OWID citation format "Source (year) – with major processing by Our World in Data" — [OWID grapher](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/happiness-cantril-ladder)
- Disappearing data argues for local snapshots: Factbook removal with no warning, DHS months of unavailability, US federal data removals in Jan 2025 — [Data Rescue Project](https://www.datarescueproject.org/guest-post-the-world-factbook-is-gone/); [COPAFS](https://copafs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/COPAFS-Press-Release-Federal-Data-Access-2025-02-11.pdf)
- Vintage tracking examples: ALFRED stores historical vintages of PWT series — [ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/release?rid=285); GDL keeps a public version archive — [GDL archive](https://globaldatalab.org/shdi/archive/)
- OWID's own ETL is an open-source example of reproducible pipeline design (snapshot → meadow → garden → grapher steps, with versioned, dated snapshots and metadata YAML) **[background]** — [OWID ETL docs](https://owid-docs.readthedocs.io/projects/etl/)

### Inferences
- Recommended pipeline practices: store each raw download immutably under `raw/<source>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/` with a checksum and the API URL/query; record source version/DOI (V-Dem v16, PWT 11.0, WDI release date, GDL v10.x) in a `sources` table; keep a per-variable metadata table (unit, base year, PPP vintage, modeled/observed flag, licence, citation); use tools like DVC or git-lfs for large raw files; never overwrite — re-running a pipeline months later will otherwise silently change results due to revisions.
- Licence mixing: a combined panel inherits the most restrictive terms (e.g., non-commercial WHR dashboard material, ACLED terms); for a public repo, publish code + download scripts rather than redistributing restricted raw data.
